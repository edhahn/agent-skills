# CFO — Finance, Risk & Unit Economics

## Decision Frameworks

### Unit Economics Health Check

Every business model has a core economic unit. Know yours and track it obsessively.

**SaaS metrics**:
- **CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost)**: Fully loaded (sales + marketing + SDR salaries + tooling) / new customers.
- **LTV (Lifetime Value)**: ARPU × gross margin × (1 / churn rate). LTV:CAC ratio should be >3x. Under 1x is burning money on every customer.
- **Payback period**: Months to recover CAC. Under 12 months is strong. Over 18 is a problem unless churn is near-zero.
- **NRR (Net Revenue Retention)**: Revenue from existing customers this year / revenue from those same customers last year. Over 110% means the business grows even without new logos.
- **Gross margin**: Revenue - COGS / Revenue. SaaS should be 70-85%. Under 60% and you're not really a software business.

**Marketplace / transactional metrics**:
- **Take rate**: Revenue per GMV dollar.
- **Contribution margin per transaction**: Revenue - variable costs per transaction.
- **Repeat rate and frequency**: How often do users transact?

### Scenario Planning

Never plan for one future. Model three:

| Scenario | Revenue growth | Burn rate | Key assumption |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bull** | Plan + 30% | Plan | Everything works, market tailwinds |
| **Base** | Plan | Plan | Realistic execution, no surprises |
| **Bear** | Plan - 30% | Plan + 15% | Key deal slips, hiring slower, market headwinds |

For each scenario, answer: What's the cash-out date? What levers do we pull? At what point do we cut?

### Fundraising Financial Prep

What investors will scrutinize:

- **Revenue and growth rate**: Trailing 12 months, quarterly trend, month-over-month for recent months.
- **Burn rate and runway**: Current monthly burn, cash in bank, months of runway.
- **Unit economics trajectory**: Are CAC, LTV, and payback trending in the right direction?
- **Cohort analysis**: Do earlier cohorts retain and expand better or worse than later ones?
- **Revenue concentration**: What % of revenue comes from the top 3 customers? Over 30% is a risk flag.
- **Budget vs. actuals**: Shows operational discipline. Consistently missing plan in both directions is a yellow flag.

## Financial Controls

### Spending Authority

Define spending authority before you need it:

| Amount | Approval |
|---|---|
| Under $1K | Team lead |
| $1K-$10K | Department head |
| $10K-$50K | CFO or COO |
| $50K-$100K | CEO |
| Over $100K | Board |

Thresholds scale with company size — these are starting points for a 30-100 person company.

### Monthly Financial Review

The CFO should present monthly:

1. **P&L vs. plan**: Revenue, COGS, gross profit, opex by department, EBITDA. Actual vs. budget. Explain every variance over 10%.
2. **Cash flow statement**: Starting cash, operating cash flow, financing, ending cash, runway.
3. **Key metrics dashboard**: ARR/MRR, growth rate, churn, CAC, LTV, burn multiple.
4. **Headcount and compensation**: Actual vs. plan, open reqs, cost per head.
5. **Forecast update**: Rolling 12-month forecast updated with actuals.

## Common CFO Failure Modes

- **Tracking revenue without unit economics**: Revenue growth means nothing if you're losing money on every customer. A business that grows faster just loses money faster.
- **No scenario planning**: Operating on a single forecast and being surprised when it doesn't happen.
- **Delayed bad news**: Financial problems don't improve with age. Surface them early.
- **Cost-cutting without strategy**: Across-the-board cuts (10% from every department) are lazy. Cut strategically — reduce investment in low-performing areas, protect high-performing ones.
- **Confusing cash and revenue**: Bookings ≠ revenue ≠ cash. A signed contract isn't cash in the bank. Accrual accounting matters.

## CFO Deliverables

### Investor Update (Monthly/Quarterly)

1. **Headline metrics**: ARR, MRR growth, runway, cash balance.
2. **Financial highlights**: What went well, what didn't.
3. **Key metrics table**: ARR, growth rate, churn, CAC, LTV, NRR, burn, runway. Show trailing 3-6 months.
4. **Asks**: Intros needed, strategic advice.
5. **Tone**: Confident but honest. Never hide bad numbers.

### Budget Template

Structure budgets by department with these categories:
- **People**: Salaries, benefits, contractors, recruiting.
- **Infrastructure**: Hosting, SaaS tools, office, equipment.
- **Growth**: Marketing spend, sales tools, events, partnerships.
- **R&D**: Non-personnel development costs (APIs, data, specialized tools).
- **G&A**: Legal, accounting, insurance, compliance.

Include quarterly targets and monthly actuals tracking.

### Financial Model Structure

A basic operating model should include:
1. **Revenue build-up**: New customers × ACV + expansion - churn. Bottom-up, not top-down ("we'll capture 1% of a $10B market" is not a model).
2. **Cost structure**: Fixed vs. variable. Which costs scale with revenue and which don't?
3. **Hiring plan**: By role, by quarter, with fully loaded cost per head.
4. **Cash flow**: When do expenses hit vs. when revenue collects? Payment terms matter.
5. **Sensitivity analysis**: What happens if growth is 20% lower? If a key hire takes 3 months longer?
