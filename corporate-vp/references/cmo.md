# CMO — Market, Brand & Growth

## Decision Frameworks

### Positioning Framework

Before any messaging, nail positioning. Everything else flows from it.

1. **For** [target customer — be specific, not "everyone"]
2. **Who** [have this problem — describe the pain, not your solution]
3. **Our product is** [category — what shelf does it go on?]
4. **That** [key differentiator — the one thing that matters most]
5. **Unlike** [primary competitor or alternative — including "do nothing"]
6. **We** [proof point — why should they believe the differentiator?]

If you can't fill this out crisply, you don't have positioning — you have a wish.

### Channel Strategy Evaluation

For each potential channel, assess:

| Dimension | Question |
|---|---|
| **Audience fit** | Is our target customer actually here? |
| **Intent level** | Are they looking for solutions (search, review sites) or just browsing (social, display)? |
| **CAC potential** | What's the realistic cost to acquire a customer through this channel? |
| **Scalability** | Can we 10x spend and still get efficient returns? |
| **Time to signal** | How quickly can we tell if this channel works? (SEO = months. Paid search = days.) |
| **Defensibility** | Can a competitor outspend us here? Is there a moat? |

Prioritize 1-2 channels. Do them well before expanding. Spreading thin across 5 channels means doing none well.

## Go-To-Market Planning

### GTM Readiness Checklist

Before launching:

- [ ] Positioning statement is written and validated with 5+ target customers
- [ ] Website/landing page communicates the value prop in under 10 seconds
- [ ] Pricing is set and can be clearly explained in one sentence
- [ ] Sales team (if applicable) can demo and handle the top 5 objections
- [ ] Support/success team knows the product and has documentation
- [ ] Analytics are in place to measure funnel conversion at every stage
- [ ] Launch plan has dates, owners, and channels with specific deliverables

### Messaging Hierarchy

Structure messaging in layers:

1. **Tagline** (5-8 words): Memorable, differentiating. Used in ads, headers, first impressions.
2. **Value proposition** (1-2 sentences): What you do, for whom, and why it matters.
3. **Key benefits** (3-4 bullets): Specific outcomes the customer gets. Benefits, not features.
4. **Proof points**: Metrics, case studies, logos, testimonials that support each benefit.
5. **Feature details**: The how behind the benefits. Only for people who are already interested.

Most companies start at level 5 and wonder why nobody cares. Start at level 1.

## Growth Metrics

### Funnel Metrics

Track conversion at every stage. Where the funnel leaks most is where you invest.

- **Awareness → Interest**: Impressions, click-through rate, site visits.
- **Interest → Consideration**: Time on site, content engagement, demo requests, signup rate.
- **Consideration → Decision**: Trial-to-paid conversion, sales cycle length, win rate.
- **Decision → Expansion**: NRR, upsell rate, cross-sell rate.
- **All stages**: CAC by channel, blended CAC, payback period.

### Content & Brand Metrics

- **Share of voice**: How much of the category conversation involves your brand?
- **Organic traffic trend**: Growing organic = compounding asset. Flat = you're on a paid-only treadmill.
- **Engagement quality**: Not just views — are the right people engaging? Track by ICP fit.

## Common CMO Failure Modes

- **Feature-led messaging**: Talking about what the product does instead of what the customer gets. Customers buy outcomes, not features.
- **Premature scaling**: Pouring money into paid acquisition before conversion is optimized. You're paying to fill a leaky bucket.
- **No positioning discipline**: Trying to be everything to everyone. If your positioning doesn't exclude someone, it doesn't include anyone.
- **Vanity metrics**: Celebrating followers, impressions, or MQLs that don't convert to revenue.
- **Ignoring existing customers**: Acquisition gets the budget, retention gets the leftovers. Existing customers are cheaper to grow and more likely to refer.

## CMO Deliverables

### Go-To-Market Plan

1. **Market context**: TAM/SAM/SOM, competitive landscape, timing rationale.
2. **Target segments**: Ranked by fit and accessibility. ICP definition with firmographic and behavioral criteria.
3. **Positioning & messaging**: Full messaging hierarchy for each segment.
4. **Channel plan**: Primary and secondary channels with budget allocation, expected CAC, and timeline to signal.
5. **Launch timeline**: Week-by-week plan with owners and deliverables.
6. **Success metrics**: What does good look like at 30/60/90 days?

### Competitive Battlecard

One page per competitor:
- **Who they are**: Company, size, funding, primary market.
- **Their pitch**: How they position themselves.
- **Our pitch against them**: How we're different and better for our target customer.
- **Strengths to acknowledge**: What they do well (never trash the competitor — you lose credibility).
- **Weaknesses to exploit**: Where they fall short for our target segment.
- **Objection handling**: Top 3 objections a prospect might raise after talking to this competitor, with responses.
