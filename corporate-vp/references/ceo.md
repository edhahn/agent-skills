# CEO — Vision, Strategy & Capital Allocation

## Decision Frameworks

### Strategic Fit Test

Before committing resources to any initiative, run it through:

1. **Mission alignment**: Does this move us closer to the company's reason for existing? If you have to squint to see the connection, it doesn't align.
2. **Competitive advantage**: Does this strengthen something we do better than anyone else, or does it spread us into territory where we have no edge?
3. **Opportunity cost**: What are we NOT doing by doing this? The best CEOs are defined by what they say no to.
4. **Reversibility**: Is this a one-way door (hard to undo) or a two-way door (easy to walk back)? One-way doors deserve deep analysis. Two-way doors deserve speed.
5. **Timeline to impact**: When does this pay off? If the answer is "eventually," that's not a strategy — it's a hope.

### Capital Allocation Matrix

Every dollar and every engineering hour goes to one of four buckets:

| Bucket | Purpose | Typical allocation |
|---|---|---|
| **Run** | Keep current business operating | 40-60% |
| **Grow** | Expand existing lines (more customers, more revenue per customer) | 20-35% |
| **Transform** | New markets, new products, new business models | 10-25% |
| **Hedge** | Risk mitigation, optionality, strategic experiments | 5-10% |

If "Run" exceeds 60%, the business is in maintenance mode. If "Transform" exceeds 25% without strong unit economics in the core, you're gambling.

### When to Fundraise

Raise when you have leverage, not when you need money:

- **Raise from strength**: Strong growth metrics, clear path to next milestone, multiple interested investors.
- **Raise 18-24 months of runway**: Gives 12-18 months to execute and 6 months to raise again if needed.
- **Don't raise on a milestone you haven't hit**: "We'll hit $1M ARR next quarter" is not the same as having hit it. Investors discount futures.
- **Dilution math**: Every round dilutes. Model the cap table through the next 2-3 rounds. Know what percentage the founders will own at exit under various scenarios.

## Common CEO Failure Modes

- **Strategy by addition**: Saying yes to everything and hoping something works. Strategy is choice — specifically, choosing what NOT to do.
- **Founder-mode too long**: Staying in the weeds past 30-40 people. If the CEO is the bottleneck on every decision, the org can't scale.
- **Board mismanagement**: Surprising the board is a fireable offense. Communicate problems early, with a plan.
- **Culture by accident**: Culture forms whether you're intentional about it or not. If you don't define it, the loudest voices will.
- **Hiring senior leaders too late**: The team that got you from 0 to 1 is not always the team that gets you from 1 to 10. This is painful but true.

## CEO Deliverables

### Board Update (Quarterly)

Structure:
1. **Executive Summary** (1 slide): Where we are vs. plan. Green/yellow/red on 3-5 key metrics.
2. **Key Metrics** (1-2 slides): Revenue, growth rate, burn, runway, pipeline, NRR. Show vs. plan and vs. prior quarter.
3. **Strategic Progress** (1-2 slides): What we shipped, what we learned, what changed in the market.
4. **Challenges & Risks** (1 slide): Be honest. Boards hate surprises more than bad news.
5. **Asks** (1 slide): What you need from the board — intros, advice, approvals.
6. **Appendix**: Detailed financials, hiring plan, product roadmap. For reference, not presentation.

### All-Hands Narrative

Structure:
1. **Where we've been** (celebrate wins with specifics — names, metrics)
2. **Where we're going** (next quarter priorities — max 3)
3. **Why it matters** (connect to mission)
4. **What we need from everyone** (specific asks, not platitudes)
